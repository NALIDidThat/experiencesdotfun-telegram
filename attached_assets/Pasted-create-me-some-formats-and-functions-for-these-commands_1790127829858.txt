Absolutely. For these four, I’d design them as high-frequency product loops, not just Telegram commands. The command should be the entry point, then the Mini-App handles the richer interaction. Humanity has suffered enough from 19-step Telegram forms.

Functionality behind /create

The backend should create:

Experience
├── ID
├── Creator
├── Organisation / Node
├── Title
├── Description
├── Category
├── Location
├── Date
├── Time
├── Capacity
├── XP Reward
├── Verification Method
├── Participants
├── Status
├── Created At
└── Impact Metrics

Important: /create should eventually distinguish between:

Community-created experiences
Organisation-created experiences
Sponsored experiences
Recurring experiences

That distinction becomes very important for trust and monetisation.

Key product principle

Don't make users search unless they want to.

The default /experiences experience should answer:

"What can I actually do today?"

That's much stronger than:

"Here's a database of 4,000 events. Good luck, citizen."

Important distinction

I'd use "Friends" for bilateral relationships and "Followers" for one-way relationships.

That gives you:

Friend
→ mutual relationship

Follow
→ one-way interest

Community
→ shared membership

Organisation
→ institutional relationship

This becomes useful later for your recommendation engine.
